## Course overview

This introductory course explored how generative AI works, what it can and cannot do, how generative AI projects are developed, and how the technology affects businesses and society.

## What I learned

- Common generative AI use cases, including writing, reading, and conversational applications
- The capabilities and limitations of generative AI
- The lifecycle of a generative AI project
- The differences between prompting, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and fine-tuning
- How to analyze individual job tasks for automation or augmentation potential
- Business considerations, societal concerns, and responsible AI

## Connection to my career goals

The most useful takeaway was learning to evaluate work at the task level instead of assuming that an entire role should be automated. This connects directly to my interest in process automation: first understand the business process, then decide whether a task should be automated, supported by AI, or kept human-led.

The course also helped me understand that generative AI should be selected based on the problem being solved. Prompting, RAG, and fine-tuning are different options with different levels of effort and complexity.
